Poem 1

Ague

On idle, humid, summer days, especially after rainstorms, I wonder if I’m vermin.

An itch-inducing insect.

When the day is as dull as dishwater and the night is moonless and still, I hear the quiet buzzing of mosquito wings.

A bored hunger overtakes me.

Try to brush me off or reason me away.

I doubt I’ll be gone for long without a direct hit. I’m just hungry.

And at night, a painful expression marks my face.

I know that I would swat my hand through the mucky air, too.

I can’t stand the buzzing and itching.

I could tell you I’ll stop biting, or spray myself with DEET.

Will I get what I crave in more abundance? What a parasitic thought.

Maybe it’s because my Papaw studied entomology.

But I’ve seen who feeds on the nectar of the lilac blooms at dusk.

Inconspicuous, yet prolific pollinators.

So in the marsh among blossoming flowers, my stomach aches less.

The rising moon glows in the lavender sky as the sweltering day cools, and I wonder: will you only have known me by my most pestilent trait?

Do you think bothersome bites and a propensity to fall ill rid me of any good?

Golden Hour

The light tonight turned everything honey-colored. Even the gravel road looked beautiful.

We stayed until after sunset and watched sailboats disappear into the haze.

It felt like the kind of memory that would become important years later.
