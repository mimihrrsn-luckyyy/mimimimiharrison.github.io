Rubicon

Due west of Miami, hidden amongst the cypress forests of the wild Everglades, lies a small private landing strip. The morning sun radiates and reflects off the faded tarmac. Carried by the dusty air, a distant engine buzz dances with a chorus of cicadas. Rubber, untamed swamp, and the sweetness of Florida wildflowers kiss my nose through the feeling of freshly rained air. Under beating rays, my hairline drips a line of sweat that beads across my brow. My palms dampen under the heavy wooden crate of guns. At 09:00 on the south tarmac, I push the throttle and storm forward. My nose leaves the runway, followed by wheels. A steady wave washes the body. My mind slips into auto-pilot, lost in thought. To be in control again. Those two thousand hours spent over a green sea of Louisiana row crops. Parallel line. After parallel line. Pivot. After. Pivot. Monotony that sets me off. Cold, steel cases of American artillery rattle in my ear, interrupting. The altimeter breaks 3,000, as the coast grows small. Free Pan-American gunslinger.
